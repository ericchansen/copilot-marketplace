# STE source, scope, and rights

This plugin contains original guidance, examples, and evaluation cases. It does
not include the ASD-STE100 publication or dictionary. It is not endorsed,
authorized, or certified by ASD. The plugin's MIT license applies to its original
material, not ASD publications or trademarks.

## Authoritative sources

- [ASD-STE100 home page](https://www.asd-ste100.org/): current issue and ownership.
- [Official copy request](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html): obtain
  an authorized copy through the maintainer.
- [Issue 9](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf):
  January 15, 2025; writing rules, dictionary, copyright, and special usage rights.
- [Tools for STE](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STEsoftware.html): tool limitations
  and explicit non-endorsement, including AI tools.
- [STEMG AI white paper](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/WhitePaper-ASD-STE100_and_AI.pdf):
  June 2026 guidance on accountability, confidentiality, and human oversight.

Issue 9 was current when this reference was prepared. For a later task, check the
applicable source or project directive rather than assuming the latest issue is
the required one. Do not mix rule numbers or dictionary entries across editions.

## Reference-assisted review

Use this workflow only when the user requests an ASD-STE100 review:

1. Establish the required issue and any governing project terminology.
2. Identify an authorized copy and an approved processing environment. Do not
   submit a request form, upload private documents, or install a PDF tool merely
   to finish the review. Use available, authorized capabilities.
3. Read only the rules and dictionary entries necessary for the current question.
   Search locally where appropriate. Avoid loading or extracting the full
   publication into model context.
4. Record the issue, rule or entry, relevant location, and review scope. Separate
   linguistic observations from verified source-based findings.
5. Check the actual meaning, grammatical role, and context, not just whether a
   word appears in the dictionary or project glossary.
6. Preserve technical and normative meaning. If a rewrite cannot satisfy both
   meaning and a requested language constraint, report the unresolved conflict.
7. Report which parts were reviewed and which remain unassessed. A qualified
   reviewer retains responsibility for high-consequence technical content.

If no authorized copy is available, report that limitation. Do not reconstruct
dictionary entries from memory, treat a third-party summary as the standard, or
silently relabel a software-clarity review as STE review.

## Common traps

- **Dictionary-only checking:** the standard combines writing rules with
  contextual vocabulary requirements; word substitutions alone are insufficient.
- **Unrestricted project terms:** technical nouns and technical verbs must fit
  the applicable categories and context. A candidate glossary entry is not
  evidence of STE approval.
- **One limit everywhere:** Issue 9 distinguishes procedural sentences
  (20 words maximum) from descriptive sentences and notes (25 words maximum).
  Sections 5, 6, and 8 provide the relevant rules and counting details.
- **Whitespace counts:** quoted text, identifiers, measurement expressions,
  parentheses, and vertical lists need special handling under Section 8.
- **Forced active voice:** Rule 3.6 permits passive voice in descriptive writing
  when the actor is unknown. Do not invent an actor.
- **Universal risk labels:** Section 7 acknowledges industry-specific safety
  conventions. Its uppercase examples are not a universal formatting mandate.
- **Lint equals conformance:** a clean result identifies only what the configured
  checks did not flag, not all possible rule, vocabulary, or technical defects.

These are selective cautions with source locations, not an exhaustive rulebook.
Consult the [official publication](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
for precise requirements and exceptions.

## Reuse and confidentiality

Free access is not a general open redistribution license. Issue 9 reserves
copyright and restricts reproduction and publication, with specified special
usage rights. Do not assume that a user, company, or public marketplace qualifies
for those rights.

Do not commit the standard, a copied dictionary, private source documents,
extracted corpora, or model caches into this plugin. Establish applicable rights
before redistribution or shared extraction. A local copy does not by itself
authorize upload to an external model service.

For rights questions, consult the actual copyright notice and ASD. For processing
questions, follow the organization's data-handling policies and the
[STEMG AI guidance](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/WhitePaper-ASD-STE100_and_AI.pdf).
This plugin provides writing assistance, not legal advice.
